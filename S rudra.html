rudra sampraday

The Rudra Sampradaya is one of the four Vaishnava sampradayas (lineages) in Hinduism, distinguished by its worship of Vishnu and his avatars. It is traditionally linked to the deity Shiva, also known as Rudra, who is believed to have passed on knowledge from Vishnu to humanity. The sampradaya is further divided into two main branches: Vishnuswami and Vallabha, the latter being associated with the Pushtimarg tradition. Here's a more detailed breakdown:
Origin: The Rudra Sampradaya is said to originate from Lord Shiva (Rudra), who is considered the first Vaishnava (follower of Vishnu) in this lineage.
Key Figures:
* Vishnuswami: A prominent figure associated with the Rudra Sampradaya, credited with initiating a specific line of thought within the sampradaya.
* Vallabhacharya: A 15th-century philosopher and theologian who founded the Pushtimarg, a branch of the Rudra Sampradaya. He is known for his philosophy of Shuddhadvaita (pure non-dualism) and for emphasizing devotion (bhakti) to Krishna.
Philosophy: The Rudra Sampradaya primarily follows the philosophy of Shuddhadvaita, which posits that the material world is a manifestation of Brahman (the ultimate reality, identified with Vishnu) and that God and the individual soul are ultimately one, yet God remains supreme.
Practices: Followers of the Rudra Sampradaya, particularly those of the Pushtimarg, emphasize devotion to Krishna, often through temple worship, particularly of Sri Nathji (Krishna as a child).
Sub-Divisions:
* Vishnuswamis: Followers of Vishnuswami, who adhered to his specific interpretations of Shuddhadvaita.
* Vallabhas (Pushtimarg): Followers of Vallabhacharya, who emphasized divine grace (pushti) as the means to attain God's love and focused on spontaneous loving devotion (raganuga bhakti).
