shri sampraday

The term "Sri Sampradaya" generally refers to Sri Vaishnavism, a prominent Hindu tradition that reveres Vishnu as the supreme deity and Lakshmi as his divine consort. It is also known as the Ramanuja Sampradaya, named after the influential philosopher and reformer, Ramanujacharya. This tradition emphasizes devotion (bhakti) to Vishnu, along with philosophical understanding of qualified non-dualism (Visishtadvaita). Here's a more detailed explanation:
• Key Figures:Sri Vaishnavism is rooted in the teachings of the Alvars (Tamil poet-saints) and is primarily associated with the philosophical contributions of Ramanujacharya, who established the Visishtadvaita school of thought.
• Core Beliefs: Sri Vaishnavism emphasizes the importance of both knowledge and devotion in attaining liberation (moksha). Ramanuja's philosophy posits that the individual soul (jiva) and the material world (jagat) are eternally connected to and dependent on the Supreme Being (Isvara), who is understood as Vishnu with attributes (Saguna Brahman).
• Concept of Sri: The term "Sri" refers to both Lakshmi, Vishnu's consort, and also serves as a prefix denoting "sacred" or "revered". In Sri Vaishnavism, Lakshmi is seen as the divine intermediary between Vishnu and the devotee, facilitating the devotee's path to liberation.
• Worship and PracticesSri Vaishnavas engage in various forms of worship, including deity worship (archana), chanting of sacred mantras, and studying scriptures. A distinctive feature of Sri Vaishnavism is the use of a particular tilak (a mark on the forehead) made with sacred clay and natural colors.
• Geographical Spread: While Sri Vaishnavism is predominantly practiced in South India, its influence has spread throughout India and beyond, with communities found in various parts of the world.
• Relationship to Other Sampradayas: Sri Vaishnavism is one of the four major Vaishnava sampradayas (traditions) within Hinduism. It is distinct from other Vaishnava traditions like the Gaudiya Vaishnavism (associated with Chaitanya Mahaprabhu).
